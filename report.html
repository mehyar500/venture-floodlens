Zone table

How zones rank, at a glance

A / AE — High risk. Insurance required with a federally backed mortgage.

V / VE — High risk — coastal (wave action).

X (shaded) / B — Moderate risk. Insurance optional but recommended.

X (unshaded) / C — Minimal risk. Insurance optional.

D — Undetermined — not studied; treat with caution.

SFHA (high-risk) = zones beginning with A or V. Over 20% of NFIP claims come from outside the SFHA.

What the zone means

Zone AE, in plain English

AE is a 1%-annual-chance (100-year) flood area with Base Flood Elevations determined. High-risk areas face at least a 1-in-4 chance of flooding during a 30-year mortgage.

  • SFHA: yes — this is a Special Flood Hazard Area.
  • Mortgage: a federally backed loan requires flood insurance here.
  • Depth: where AH/AO-style shallow flooding applies, expect 1–3 ft.

Insurance cost range

What AE typically costs

$800–$2,000/year (estimate)

Scaled from published NFIP medians for a $250,000 building-coverage policy. Median ≈ $1,200 at that coverage; full-risk up to ~$1,750+.

Under FEMA's Risk Rating 2.0, zone is no longer the main pricing driver — distance to water, replacement cost, elevation, foundation, and claims history are. Premiums are property-specific.

Estimates based on published NFIP data (FEMA, 2025–2026). Your actual premium is set by your insurer under FEMA's Risk Rating 2.0 and varies by property. Not a quote — get a real quote at floodsmart.gov.

Questions to ask

Take this to your agent

  • What is the property's elevation relative to the Base Flood Elevation?
  • Is there an elevation certificate on file — and can I see it?
  • Has this address ever filed an NFIP claim? (Claims history follows the property.)
  • Are there any Letters of Map Change (LOMA/LOMR) affecting this lot?
  • How would the premium change with a higher deductible or less contents coverage?
