What the zone means

Zone AE, in plain English

AE is a 1%-annual-chance (100-year) flood area with Base Flood Elevations determined. High-risk areas face at least a 1-in-4 chance of flooding during a 30-year mortgage.

  • SFHA: yes — this is a Special Flood Hazard Area.
  • Mortgage: a federally backed loan requires flood insurance here.
  • Note: over 20% of NFIP claims come from outside high-risk zones — "not in a flood zone" is not "no flood risk."

Insurance cost range

What AE typically costs

$800–$2,000/year (estimate)

Scaled from published NFIP medians for a $250,000 building-coverage policy. Median ≈ $1,200 at that coverage; full-risk up to ~$1,750+.

Under FEMA's Risk Rating 2.0, zone is no longer the main pricing driver — distance to water, replacement cost, elevation, foundation, and claims history are. Premiums are property-specific.

Questions to ask

Take this to your agent

  • What is the property's elevation relative to the Base Flood Elevation?
  • Is there an elevation certificate on file — and can I see it?
  • Has this address ever filed an NFIP claim? (Claims history follows the property.)

  • How would the premium change with a higher deductible or less contents coverage?

    FAQ

    Questions buyers ask

    Is the lookup really free?

    Yes — the FEMA zone letter, the plain-words risk level, and the data vintage are free, forever, with no account. We ask for your email so we can send the full breakdown (unsubscribe anytime, one click). The $19 report is the upgrade: a 10-page PDF that explains what the zone means in dollars and what to ask your agent.

    Where does the data come from?

    Every lookup reads FEMA's National Flood Hazard Layer (NFHL) — the same public dataset behind FEMA's own Map Service Center. We never invent or interpolate a zone. If FEMA is temporarily unreachable, we say so and show the last checked data with its date — or nothing at all.

    Is this an official flood determination?

    No. This is an informational lookup — not a certified flood zone determination, and not a replacement for the Standard Flood Hazard Determination Form (SFHDF) your lender or insurer requires. Boundaries are approximate, maps change over time, and recent map amendments may not be reflected yet.

    What does "Zone AE" mean in dollars?

    Zone AE means high risk — insurance required with a federally backed mortgage. Typical NFIP cost for an AE property is roughly $800–$2,000/year (estimate, for a $250,000 building-coverage policy; median ≈ $1,200). Your actual premium is set by your insurer under FEMA's Risk Rating 2.0 and varies by property — get a real quote at floodsmart.gov.

    What if my address isn't in FEMA's maps?

    We'll tell you honestly — "no FEMA flood data for this location" — instead of faking it, and point you to FEMA's Map Service Center. Areas labeled D are possible but undetermined flood hazard (unstudied); FEMA prices in the uncertainty.

    How do I unsubscribe from emails?

    Every email carries a one-click unsubscribe link, and you can always use our unsubscribe page. One click, no login, no maze.
