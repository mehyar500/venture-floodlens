Questions everyone's asking today

What's the difference between a Flood Warning and a Flash Flood Warning?

A Flash Flood Warning is the NWS's most urgent flood alert: flash flooding ongoing or expected within minutes to hours. A Flood Warning covers slower river flooding that lasts days — the kind that closes roads all week, fills basements by inches per hour, and does the quiet damage that redraws FEMA's long-term maps. This week is the second kind.

Does living near a river put me in a high-risk FEMA zone?

Not automatically. Distance to the river matters less than where the mapped zone boundary falls — two streets a block apart can sit in different zones. The zone letter is what insurers and lenders price off, so the only way to know is to check the mapped zone for your exact address.

Can a river flood change my FEMA zone?

Repeated regional flooding is exactly what triggers FEMA map revisions. When a map is redrawn, whole neighborhoods can move into a higher-risk zone — changing quotes and potentially triggering lender flood-insurance requirements on refinance or renewal. It's already happening: Austin's 2026 FEMA map update is reclassifying some Zone X parcels to Zone AE. Check your zone now; that's your baseline before any revision.

Will river flooding near me raise my flood insurance quote?

Insurers and lenders price off the official FEMA zone map, not the news. One week of flooding doesn't rewrite your zone overnight — but under Risk Rating 2.0, premiums are priced to your property: distance to water, flood type, foundation, first-floor height.
